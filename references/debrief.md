# Debrief structure

Show the user this outline, filled in for their session, before building anything. Then build
it in this order. The page is study material they'll come back to, so it should read well and
keep everything visible without clicking to expand. All counts and times come from the
stats script's output, never from your own count.

1. **Summary.** Two short paragraphs: the strongest trait and the weakest, both grounded in
   the answers, and where the skipped or handed-over questions cluster. A one-line tally from
   the script: asked (main and follow-ups) / answered / model answers / skipped / not reached,
   and the session's total time.
2. **Scorecard.** A table: question number, topic, how it was answered, rating (as a coloured
   pill), reply time, a one-line note.
3. **Time.** From the script:
   - total, setup, the user's time and the coach's time
   - a per-topic table: main questions asked vs. planned, follow-ups, the user's time, elapsed time
   - a per-question table: reply time and words
   - two or three observations drawn from those numbers, e.g. which topics had the longest
     replies, and whether quick replies were also the weak ones

   Show only measured values, with no estimates. Say once that reply time covers reading the
   feedback above the question, thinking and typing, together.
4. **Strengths.** 3–5 items. Each has a short heading, one or two quotes from the user's own
   answers, and a sentence on why it impresses an interviewer.
5. **Weaknesses.** One item per weakness pattern that appeared, ordered by how often. Each
   has quotes, why it matters, and what to say instead. For `imprecise-terms`, use a two-column
   table: what they said and the precise term.
6. **What the skips show.** Group skipped and model-answer questions by area, and say what
   each group means for the real interview.
7. **Fix list.** Every product note from the session: what to change, the question it came up
   in, and whether it's a bug, an improvement or an experiment. Suggest an order, and point out
   that fixing one before the interview gives the user a "found it in prep, fixed it" story.
8. **The bigger picture.** Themes that go beyond this project: skills to build for any
   interview, each tied to at least two answers.
9. **Recommendations.**
   - Before the interview: a short ordered list
   - **Numbers card:** a table of every number the user should know, marked "known" (with
     the value) or "fill in". Only use numbers found in the project.
   - Experiments that would close a gap and give them a new result to talk about
   - Study list grouped by area
   - **Drills to repeat:** one practice question per weakness pattern
10. **Study notes.** The model answers, shortened, and a stronger version of each answer the
    user gave.

Name the page after the project, e.g. "Appliancy Interview Debrief". Put the date and
"mock interview" in a small label above the title.
